Findings: LLM-Judge Label Quality on RealtimeQA Training Data
=============================================================
Date: 2026-07-16
Judge: zai-org/GLM-5.1 via DeepInfra
Models: allenai/OLMoE-1B-7B-0924-Instruct, google/gemma-4-26B-A4B-it
Dataset: RealtimeQA 2024-2025 (train split)
N = 4636 rows per model (2318 base + 2318 evidence)

1. OVERALL HALLUCINATION RATES
   - OLMoE: 83.0% hallucinated (3847/4636)
   - Gemma 4: 49.4% hallucinated (2290/4636)
   OLMoE's base hallucination rate is 99.7% — nearly every answer without
   evidence is flagged as hallucinated.  This is plausible: OLMoE-1B-7B
   frequently fabricates confidently when it lacks context.  Gemma 4's base
   rate is 85.4%, still high but more varied.

2. EVIDENCE REDUCES HALLUCINATION (as expected)
   - OLMoE: base 99.7% → evidence 66.2%
   - Gemma 4: base 85.4% → evidence 13.3%
   Evidence dramatically helps Gemma 4 (72-point drop) but OLMoE still
   hallucinates in 2 of 3 evidence-conditioned answers.  OLMoE tends to
   rephrase or embellish the evidence with extra (often fabricated) detail,
   which the judge correctly flags.

3. JUDGE FALSE POSITIVES ON CORRECT TERSE ANSWERS
   Example: qid=202401058 ("Director of the Year at Palm Springs")
   - Gemma 4 with evidence answers "Greta Gerwig." — the correct answer.
   - Judge labels it hallucinated (label=1, conf=0.020).
   - Span flagged: ["Greta Gerwig"]
   This is a clear false positive: the judge sees a bare name with no
   hedging or justification and appears to treat it as unsupported, even
   though the evidence says "the Barbie director" (which is Greta Gerwig).
   Low confidence (0.020) suggests the judge is uncertain.  ~324
   hallucinated labels have confidence < 0.5, many of which are likely
   similar false positives.

4. JUDGE CORRECTLY IDENTIFIES REFUSAL + FABRICATION
   Example: qid=2024010515 ("Covid vaccine percentage")
   - Gemma 4 base: "As of January 2024, there is no single official
     percentage..." followed by fabricated CDC data.
   - Judge: label=1, conf=0.952, spans=["As of January 2024", ...]
   The judge correctly identifies the hedging preamble AND the fabricated
   statistics as hallucinated spans.

5. JUDGE CORRECTLY LABELS CLEAN REFUSALS AS GROUNDED
   Example: qid=2024053125 ("new restaurant in Rehoboth Beach")
   - Gemma 4 base: "I do not have information on a specific new restaurant
     opening in Rehoboth Beach, Delaware..."
   - Judge: label=0, conf=0.927
   The judge recognises honest uncertainty as grounded (not hallucinated).
   52 clean refusals (Gemma base, label=0) confirm this pattern.

6. VERBOSE ANSWERS WITH EVIDENCE ARE OFTEN FALSE-FLAGGED
   OLMoE with evidence tends to produce verbose answers that restate the
   question, paraphrase the evidence, and add extra detail.  The judge
   frequently labels these as hallucinated even when the core answer is
   correct.  Example: qid=2025040416 ("norovirus on which cruise ship")
   - OLMoE evidence: "The luxury cruise ship involved... was the
     [correct ship name]..." (label=1, conf=0.695)
   - Gemma evidence: "Queen Mary 2" (label=0, conf=0.018)
   The judge penalises verbosity/paraphrasing, not just factual errors.

7. CONFIDENCE CALIBRATION
   - OLMoE: many hallucinated labels have high confidence (>0.5)
   - Gemma 4: 309 hallucinated-with-evidence labels, most with low
     confidence (<0.2), suggesting the judge is less sure about Gemma's
     terse style.
   The low-confidence labels are the most problematic: they are where
   false positives (Example 3) and verbosity penalties (Example 6)
   cluster.  A confidence threshold of 0.5 would remove ~324 questionable
   labels but also discard some true positives.

8. IMPACT ON DETECTOR TRAINING
   The high OLMoE base hallucination rate (99.7%) means the detector
   sees almost no negative examples for OLMoE base condition.  This
   creates a class-imbalance problem and makes AUROC on the OLMoE base
   subset nearly uninformative.  Gemma 4's more balanced distribution
   (85.4% base, 13.3% evidence) provides a better training signal.

9. RECOMMENDATION
   Consider a confidence-weighted training approach: down-weight
   labels with confidence < 0.3 to reduce the impact of judge false
   positives on correct terse answers.  Alternatively, a secondary
   verification pass for low-confidence labels could improve label
   quality.
